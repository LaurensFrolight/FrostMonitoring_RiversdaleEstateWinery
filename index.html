How this data works

The methodology behind the numbers shown in this dashboard.
Radiation frost: calm wind + large air/surface temperature gap (clear, still nights). Advection frost: sustained wind bringing in a cold air mass. Mixed: characteristics of both. Severity (Marginal / Moderate / Severe / Critical) is based on how far surface temperature drops below 0°C.
Surface (skin) temperature — the temperature of the crop surface itself (leaf, grape, ground), not the air. This is what actually determines frost damage, and can be several degrees colder than the air on clear, calm nights due to radiative heat loss.

Air temperature — the standard temperature measured ~2m above the ground, as reported in typical weather forecasts.

Wet bulb temperature — the lowest temperature air can reach through evaporative cooling, combining temperature and humidity. It's a property of the air, not the surface — but on a wet crop surface (dew, recent rain), evaporative cooling can pull the actual surface temperature down toward this value, even if the surface model alone shows a safe reading.

Delta-T — the gap between air temperature and wet bulb temperature. A higher delta-T means drier air with more evaporative cooling potential — relevant both for frost risk and for spray timing.

Relative humidity (min–max) — how much moisture the air holds, shown as the night's range rather than an average, since it typically peaks near the coldest pre-dawn point.

Wind (avg / direction) — the night's average wind speed and prevailing direction, calculated over the same night window used for frost detection.

Precipitation — total rainfall for the full calendar day (not limited to the night window).

Data predictability — meteoblue's confidence score for the forecast itself; lower scores mean the figures above may still shift as the date approaches.
